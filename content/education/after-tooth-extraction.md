A blood clot forms in the socket soon after a tooth is removed. Almost everything in the first few days is about protecting that clot so the area can heal.

## The first 24 hours

- **Keep the gauze in place** and bite on it firmly for 30–45 minutes. Replace it with a fresh folded piece if oozing continues.
- **Don't rinse, spit or use a straw.** Suction and swishing can dislodge the clot.
- **Rest with your head raised** on an extra pillow for the first night.
- **Use a cold pack** on the cheek — 10 minutes on, 10 minutes off — to limit swelling.
- **Eat soft, cool food** such as curd, idli or mashed banana, and chew on the other side.
- **No smoking or alcohol.** Both slow healing and raise the risk of a dry socket.

## From day two

- Start **gentle warm salt-water rinses** (half a teaspoon of salt in a glass of warm water) after meals, letting the water fall out of your mouth rather than spitting hard.
- Brush your other teeth normally, and carefully near the socket.
- Take any medicines exactly as prescribed.

## What is normal

- Slight oozing for the first day
- Swelling that peaks around day two or three and then settles
- Mild stiffness of the jaw after wisdom tooth removal

## Call the clinic if you notice

- Bleeding that does not slow after 30 minutes of firm pressure on gauze
- Pain that gets **worse** after day three instead of better — this can be a sign of a dry socket
- A bad taste, pus, or fever
- Numbness of the lip or tongue that has not worn off by the next day
