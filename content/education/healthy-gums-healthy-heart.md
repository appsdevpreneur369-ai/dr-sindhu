Most people think of the mouth and the heart as unrelated. Over the past few decades, though, researchers have noticed that people with long-standing gum disease are more likely to also have heart and blood-vessel problems. As a periodontist-led clinic, this is a topic we talk about with patients every week.

## What is gum disease?

Gum disease starts when plaque — a sticky film of bacteria — builds up along the gum line. The gums react by becoming red, puffy and prone to bleeding. This early stage is called **gingivitis**, and it is usually reversible with a professional clean and better brushing.

If it continues, the inflammation can spread deeper and slowly destroy the bone that holds the teeth. This is **periodontitis**. It is often painless, which is why many people do not notice it until teeth begin to feel loose.

## How might the gums affect the heart?

Studies have found an **association** between periodontitis and conditions such as heart disease and stroke. Researchers are still working out exactly why, but a few explanations are being studied:

- **Bacteria entering the bloodstream.** Inflamed gums bleed easily, which may give mouth bacteria a route into the blood.
- **Inflammation throughout the body.** Long-term gum inflammation is linked with higher levels of inflammatory markers in the blood, and inflammation is associated with changes in the artery walls.
- **Shared risk factors.** Smoking, diabetes, stress and a poor diet raise the risk of both gum disease and heart disease.

It is important to be clear: gum disease has not been shown to directly *cause* heart disease, and treating your gums is not a treatment for a heart condition. What the evidence does suggest is that the health of your mouth and the health of the rest of your body are connected.

## Signs your gums need attention

- Gums that bleed when you brush or floss
- Red, swollen or tender gums
- Bad breath that keeps coming back
- Gums that seem to be pulling away from the teeth
- Teeth that feel loose or have started to shift

## Simple steps that help both

1. **Brush twice a day** for two minutes with a soft brush and fluoride toothpaste, angling the bristles towards the gum line.
2. **Clean between your teeth** once a day with floss or small interdental brushes.
3. **Stop smoking or chewing tobacco.** It is one of the biggest risk factors for both gum disease and heart disease.
4. **Keep diabetes under control.** Blood sugar and gum health affect each other.
5. **Have your gums checked** regularly — a gum examination measures the pockets around your teeth and can find problems before they hurt.

## If you have a heart condition

Tell your dentist about any heart condition, stents, valve replacement or blood-thinning medicines before treatment. Some procedures may need planning with your physician. Never stop a prescribed medicine on your own before a dental visit.

*Taking care of your smile is one more way of looking after your heart.*
