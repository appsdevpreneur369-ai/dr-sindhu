Root canal treatment removes the inflamed or infected pulp from inside a tooth and seals the canals. The ache that brought you in usually eases quickly, but the tooth needs some care while it settles.

## Straight after the appointment

- **Wait until the numbness wears off** before eating, so you don't bite your cheek or tongue.
- If you have a **temporary filling**, avoid sticky and very hard food on that side. A thin layer may wear away; that is expected.

## The next few days

- Some **tenderness when biting** is common for a few days as the surrounding tissues calm down.
- A pain reliever you normally take safely can help; use it as directed on the pack or by your dentist.
- **Keep brushing and flossing** normally, including around the treated tooth.

## Why a crown is often recommended

A tooth that has had root canal treatment has usually already lost a lot of structure to decay or old fillings, and it can become more brittle. Back teeth take strong chewing forces, so a **crown** is usually advised to cover and protect the tooth from cracking. Your dentist will tell you whether your tooth needs one and when.

## Call the clinic if

- Pain gets worse after two or three days instead of better
- Swelling appears inside the mouth or on the face
- The temporary filling falls out completely
- Your bite feels high or uneven
